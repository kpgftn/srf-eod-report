End-of-Day Report · Tuesday, 23 June 2026

Trust became an operating problem.

Twenty sessions pushed past whether AI, tokenisation and programmable money work, into who authorises an agent before it acts, what money settles with finality, and how institutions retain control amid concentrated tech suppliers. New infrastructure, standards, consortia and supervisory tools multiplied, but independently verified outcomes stayed scarce. The day's real message: finance has not finished rewiring itself. Governance, monetary architecture and credible measurement will decide whether that rewiring can move safely from pilots into production at scale.

Featured Sessions — Tuesday, 23 June 2026

09:00 · Fireside

Opening Fireside Chat: Karin Keller-Sutter, Federal Councillor, Switzerland & H.E. Maria Luís Albuquerque, Commissioner, European Commission

Crisis decisions must be made before full information arrives; targeted state backstops were defended as system-preserving, not institution-rescuing. Resilience now hinges on stronger domestic buffers versus deeper European market integration reducing reliance on bank lending.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The opening conversation used the 2023 Credit Suisse collapse and an earlier Portuguese banking crisis to examine crisis decisions made with incomplete information. Targeted state backstops were defended as measures to preserve the system rather than rescue one institution. The discussion then moved from emergency response to structural reform: Switzerland’s still-unfinished liquidity-backstop legislation, the contested proposal to fully capitalise foreign subsidiaries of systemically important banks, and Europe’s effort to build deeper capital markets. The central tension was whether resilience comes primarily from stronger domestic buffers or from a more integrated European market that reduces corporate reliance on bank lending.

09:30 · Keynote

Keynote Speech by Alvin Tan, Minister of State, Singapore

AI, tokenisation and quantum computing converge on one dependency: trust, now backed by new risk-management toolkits, an agentic-AI supervisory group and fresh quantum funding. Priorities are interoperable infrastructure, cryptographic trust for agentic payments, and quantum-resilient systems before migration turns urgent.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The keynote framed AI, tokenisation and quantum computing as three converging shifts whose common dependency is trust. Recent public initiatives were offered as evidence that the conversation is moving into implementation: an AI risk-management toolkit, an agentic-AI working group, funding for quantum and AI capability, wholesale-tokenisation experiments and new stablecoin legislation. Three practical priorities followed: scale interoperable public-private utilities, establish legal and cryptographic trust architecture for agentic payments, and make tokenised infrastructure quantum-resilient before migration becomes urgent. The argument was less about choosing a winning technology than ensuring the foundations can support all three safely.

10:30 · Keynote

The Global State of SupTech: Keynote by Marlene Amstad, Chair, FINMA

A two-model inspection assistant and a new digital-asset exposure dashboard show supervisory AI moving into daily use, even as AI-enabled fraud grows sharply more profitable. Such tools should widen detection, never transfer decision authority from accountable supervisors.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The SupTech keynote paired a global supervisory survey with tools already operating or in development. An inspection assistant uses one model to identify material, a second model to check it and a human supervisor to retain judgment and responsibility. A live dashboard is also being used to monitor digital-asset exposure. The counterweight is AI-enhanced fraud, presented as materially more profitable than traditional methods. A new international sprint has begun developing crypto-market supervision tools, with a build phase scheduled for September. The practical lesson was that supervisory AI should expand attention and detection without transferring decision authority away from accountable people.

11:55 · Panel

The Agentic Leap: Deploying AI at Scale in Financial Services

Competing failure claims met live examples of production workflows, including a reported cut in false-positive screening hits, as debate shifted to the stack needed to scale. The agreement: probabilistic systems propose or detect, deterministic policy or people approve.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The panel challenged the assumption that agentic AI remains trapped in demonstrations. Competing failure statistics were met with examples of live bank workflows, including a reported reduction in false-positive screening hits. The discussion then widened to the stack required for scale: compute, model ownership, policy engines, application design and institutional controls. Trust was described as something earned incrementally rather than declared at launch. The most durable point of agreement was a division of labour between probabilistic systems that propose or detect and deterministic policies or accountable people that approve. Production maturity remains uneven, but the governance architecture is becoming more concrete.

14:00 · Policy dialogue

Policymakers' Dialogue: Forging Europe's Strategic Autonomy in Finance

The dialogue asked whether Europe treats financial-infrastructure resilience with the urgency applied to defence, noting supplier concentration reflects a deliberate efficiency trade-off. The disagreement: one view weighed redundancy's cost, another warned sovereignty can tip into protectionism without openness.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The policy dialogue considered whether Europe has treated financial-infrastructure resilience with the urgency now applied to defence. National payment infrastructure and rapidly increased defence spending were used to show that small states can move decisively. Digital supplier concentration was framed as an efficiency trade-off rather than an accidental condition: reducing it can require institutions to pay more for European alternatives. The central disagreement concerned the meaning of autonomy. One view prioritised the cost of redundancy; another warned that sovereignty can become protectionism unless it remains anchored in openness. Resilience therefore requires both credible alternatives and discipline about when independence is worth its price.

15:05 · Primer panel

Tokenization 101 for Finance: What's Being Built, How, and Why it Matters

Tokenisation has moved from terminology to production infrastructure, with real assets, repo markets and collateral mobility now live. Yet settlement money to match tokenised assets remains the persistent gap, so activity still outpaces provable scale.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The primer moved beyond tokenisation terminology to production infrastructure and operating use cases. Examples included large cumulative payment volumes, on-chain real-world assets, repo markets and collateral mobility. The recurring infrastructure gap was between tokenised assets and the money required to settle them. Strong agreement made this a useful baseline session rather than a debate: institutions are moving, recognisable asset managers are entering the market and native on-chain issuance is becoming the next test. The key distinction was between activity and scale. Large announced or cumulative figures do not by themselves show broad liquidity, repeat use or interoperable settlement across institutions.

17:45 · Announcement

Transforming Global FX Markets

A new direct settlement corridor between regulated euro and won stablecoins would let two major trading partners bypass the dollar, using atomic payment-versus-payment settlement compatible with existing systems. Its significance depends on regulatory approval, live issuance and real transaction volume.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The closing announcement introduced Project Pangea, a proposed direct foreign-exchange settlement corridor between regulated euro and Korean-won stablecoins. Bank consortia on each side would support atomic payment-versus-payment settlement while preserving compatibility with existing messaging and core systems. The first corridor targets a major bilateral trade relationship that still relies heavily on the United States dollar as a vehicle currency. The concept addresses regulatory fragmentation and operational complexity identified in an earlier Korea–Japan experiment. Its significance will depend on execution: regulatory approval, live issuance, transaction volume, liquidity and whether direct settlement produces measurable savings without creating new fragmentation between bank-led stablecoin networks.

12:00 · Roundtable

Agentic Commerce: Who Governs the Machine that Pays?

The dialogue examined what changes when software agents initiate payments without a person approving each transaction, and found governance shifting upstream — to who authorises an agent, what it may buy, and who is liable when it errs. It matters because existing payment regulation assumes a human knowingly authorises every transaction, an assumption several emerging standards for verified intent and machine payments are now trying to replace, even as questions remain over whether human review can scale and which rails will carry near-term volume.

A formal policy report on this dialogue follows separately.

Read full summaryClose full summary

The roundtable asked what changes when software agents initiate payments without a person approving each transaction. Governance shifts upstream to the mandate: who authorised the agent, what it may buy, which evidence of intent travels with the payment and who is liable when the outcome is wrong. Several standards and protocol efforts are already addressing verified intent, transaction mandates and machine payments. Two questions remained unresolved. Human-in-the-loop review may not be meaningful at hundreds of thousands of transactions per day, and it is still unclear whether card rails, stablecoins or a mixture will carry most near-term agent-driven volume.
